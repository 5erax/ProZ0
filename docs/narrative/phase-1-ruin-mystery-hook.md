# 3. INFORMATION CLASSIFICATION

## CONFIRMED

The following are Phase 1 narrative facts within the authority and scope of #65:

1. **The ruin is not a current human landing/colony artifact.**  
   It existed before the current human arrival and cannot reasonably be mistaken for something built by the present expedition.

2. **The ruin is engineered evidence of an earlier technological presence.**  
   It is not merely an unusual natural formation. Its existence demonstrates intentional construction or manufacture by unknown builders.

3. **The builders are absent from the Phase 1 encounter.**  
   The player finds evidence of prior presence, not a living representative, active faction, or explained society.

4. **The site does not explain itself.**  
   Its original purpose, operation, cultural role, and larger context remain unknown after investigation.

5. **The builders' identity remains unresolved.**  
   Phase 1 does not establish whether they were alien, human-related, descendants of another human population, or something else.

6. **Their fate remains unresolved.**  
   The ruin does not prove extinction, evacuation, destruction, migration, dormancy, or any specific catastrophe.

7. **The Ancient Alloy Shard is physical evidence associated with the ruin.**  
   It is a recoverable sample tied to the discovery, but Phase 1 does not assign it a decoded function, extraordinary capability, faction identity, or endgame meaning.

8. **The first ruin is evidence, not exposition.**  
   The player should leave with stronger certainty that an earlier technological presence existed and stronger uncertainty about who they were and what happened.

9. **The emotional target remains hopeful, lonely, curious, and occasionally tense.**  
   The ruin should deepen wonder and isolation without turning the game into horror, prophecy, or a mandatory combat narrative.

10. **The discovery must create a reason to continue the same persistent world.**  
    It is a beginning of inquiry, not a solved chapter or campaign-ending reveal.

# 4. PHASE 1 NARRATIVE CORE

The Phase 1 ruin should communicate one clean proposition:

> **Someone built here before us. They are not here to explain it.**

That proposition is the narrative payload of the first discovery.

The ruin is not a lore encyclopedia, a faction introduction, a prophecy, a boss arena, or a tutorial for an alien-language system. It is the first hard contradiction to the colonists' initial assumption that this is merely an unknown natural world.

Before the ruin, the planet can be strange because it is unfamiliar.

After the ruin, the planet is strange because **someone was here before humanity's current arrival**.

That change in interpretation is the Phase 1 mystery payoff.

---

# 5. WHAT THE PLAYER SHOULD UNDERSTAND AFTER INVESTIGATION

After the first valid investigation, a new player should be able to explain the following without needing a full lore exposition:

1. **This place was made intentionally.**
2. **It was here before the current human colony arrived.**
3. **Its makers had technological capability sufficient to leave engineered structures/material behind.**
4. **The makers are not present to explain the site.**
5. **The colony does not yet know what the ruin was for.**
6. **The colony does not yet know who the makers were or what happened to them.**
7. **The Ancient Alloy Shard is tangible evidence that the discovery is real, not speculation.**
8. **There is now a meaningful reason to keep exploring this same world.**

The player does **not** need to understand any civilization name, faction map, historical timeline, language, religion, political structure, species biology, cause of collapse, or endgame truth.

---

# 6. PRIMARY MYSTERY HOOK

The central Phase 1 mystery question is:

> **If this place was built before us, where did its builders go—and what else did they leave behind?**

This question intentionally contains two scales of curiosity:

- **Immediate:** What happened at or around this abandoned site?
- **Long-form:** What does the wider planet reveal about the civilization that preceded the colony?

Phase 1 answers neither question.

The player's reason to continue is not a quest order imposed by this brief. It is a changed understanding of the world: the persistent map now contains proof that the colony has arrived inside an unfinished history.

# 7. ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING BEATS

These are **narrative meaning requirements**, not prescribed layouts or art direction. World/Level Design and Art may choose the exact spatial and visual expression.

## Beat 1 — Recognition: "This is constructed"

**Must communicate:**  
The site has enough intentional order that the player can distinguish it from ordinary terrain or a natural anomaly.

**Narrative function:**  
Move the player from uncertainty to the conclusion that the feature was made.

**Must not imply:**  
A specific culture, species, religion, faction, architectural school, or final visual language.

---

## Beat 2 — Temporal separation: "This was here before us"

**Must communicate:**  
Nothing about the site should read as a recent extension of the current landing party. The discovery belongs to an earlier presence on the planet.

**Narrative function:**  
Separate the ruin from human colony progress and establish historical depth.

**Must not imply:**  
An exact age, date, era length, or chronology that has not been approved.

---

## Beat 3 — Absence: "The builders are not here"

**Must communicate:**  
The site is encountered without an active builder population explaining or operating it for the player.

**Narrative function:**  
Create loneliness and the first meaningful gap in the planet's history.

**Must not imply:**  
That the builders are confirmed extinct, dead, evacuated, hostile, trapped, or destroyed.

---

## Beat 4 — Intent without interpretation: "It had a purpose; we do not know it"

**Must communicate:**  
The ruin contains enough deliberate structure or organization to imply function, but the current colony cannot confidently identify that function from this one site.

**Narrative function:**  
Create curiosity through partial legibility rather than through arbitrary obscurity.

**Must not imply:**  
That the ruin is specifically a temple, weapon, laboratory, archive, beacon, portal, factory, terraformer, tomb, prison, or vehicle.

---

## Beat 5 — Material evidence: "We can bring something back"

**Must communicate:**  
The Ancient Alloy Shard is physically associated with the ruin and serves as a concrete sample of the earlier engineered presence.

**Narrative function:**  
Turn the mystery from an impression into portable evidence while preserving the approved inventory/death/recovery loop.

**Must not imply:**  
A decoded technology, supernatural property, immediate crafting breakthrough, research tree, or unique power not already approved elsewhere.

---

## Beat 6 — The unanswered horizon: "The site is proof, not the whole story"

**Must communicate:**  
Investigating the ruin closes the question "was someone here before us?" but opens larger questions about identity, purpose, fate, and wider planetary history.

**Narrative function:**  
End the Phase 1 discovery on curiosity rather than completion.

**Must not require:**  
Another ruin, quest marker, cutscene, NPC transmission, translated record, or new mechanic inside the vertical slice.

---

# 8. PLAYER-FACING DISCOVERY MEANING

Exact UI copy, timing, typography, animation, and notification treatment are outside this role's authority.

Whatever presentation is used, the **semantic payload** of the INVESTIGATED discovery should be equivalent to:

- **Not ours:** the structure is not from the present human landing.
- **Older:** it predates the current colony.
- **Engineered:** this is evidence of intentional technological construction.
- **Absent:** whoever made it is not here to explain it.
- **Unknown:** purpose, identity, and fate remain unresolved.
- **Continue:** the planet now contains a mystery worth investigating further.

A short narrative phrasing may be derived downstream from that payload, but no downstream implementation should upgrade uncertainty into fact.

---

# 9. ANCIENT ALLOY SHARD — NARRATIVE BOUNDARY

For Phase 1, `Ancient Alloy Shard` should be treated as a **human field designation**, not a translated builder term.

It confirms only that:

- the sample is associated with the ruin;
- it is treated by the current colony as an unfamiliar engineered material/sample;
- it can be physically recovered under the already-approved gameplay rules.

It does **not** confirm:

- exact chemical composition;
- age in years;
- energy properties;
- intelligence or data storage;
- military purpose;
- cultural meaning;
- builder species;
- faction ownership;
- a crafting/research unlock;
- a direct explanation of the civilization's disappearance.

Future tasks may define those properties only under their own approved authority and source-of-truth constraints.

# 11. TONE GUARDRAILS

## Hopeful

The discovery should expand the colony's sense of possibility and knowledge. It is proof that the planet has history, not proof that humanity is doomed.

## Lonely

The strongest emotional signal is absence: evidence of intelligence without anyone present to answer questions.

## Curious

The ruin should reward attention with a better question, not a giant exposition dump.

## Occasionally tense

Tension comes from reaching an unknown site during an expedition and confronting uncertainty. The ruin itself does not need to become a horror reveal, trap, boss encounter, or mandatory threat.

## Avoid

- cosmic-horror certainty;
- prophecy or chosen-one framing;
- immediate existential-war framing;
- cynical "everything before us failed, therefore we will too" messaging;
- comedic treatment that trivializes the first proof of prior civilization;
- grandiose exposition that claims more than the evidence supports.

# 12. FUTURE-CANON INVARIANTS

Later narrative expansion may answer the open questions, but it must remain compatible with the following Phase 1 facts unless a higher-precedence source explicitly changes them:

1. The Phase 1 ruin predates the current human landing.
2. The ruin is intentionally engineered.
3. It proves an earlier technological presence.
4. The builders are not identified during Phase 1.
5. The builders' fate is not established during Phase 1.
6. The ruin's original purpose is not established during Phase 1.
7. The Ancient Alloy Shard does not by itself decode the civilization.
8. The first discovery leaves the player with a continuing planet mystery.

A later reveal may reinterpret incomplete evidence, but it should not casually turn the ruin into:
- a natural formation;
- an object secretly built by the current landing party;
- a fully understood site that the colony inexplicably failed to recognize;
- proof of a specific faction/species that Phase 1 was supposed to leave unresolved.

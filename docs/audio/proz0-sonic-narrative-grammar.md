# 1. PURPOSE

The purpose of this grammar is to let future audio work support ProZ0's nonlinear mystery without turning sound into an omniscient narrator.

The player should be able to hear:
- that two places may be related;
- that a place feels human, natural, engineered, altered, incomplete, reused, or uncertain;
- that a repeated sonic relationship has changed context;
- that later evidence complicates an earlier interpretation;

without audio declaring:
- who the prior builders were;
- why they acted;
- what the Great Dispersal "really" was;
- whether Something Older is a separate civilization, intelligence, technology, or supernatural force;
- what moral conclusion the player should draw.

The core sonic design principle is:

> **Sound may reveal relationship, process, contrast, and uncertainty. It must not reveal unresolved truth before the narrative evidence does.**

# 2. APPROVED FOUNDATION TRANSLATED INTO AUDIO

## 2.1 Humanity rebuilding / Seed Colony

**APPROVED FOUNDATION**

Humanity is rebuilding civilizational capability after catastrophe. The Seed Colony / Civilization Recovery framing is core.

**AUDIO GRAMMAR**

Human settlement sound should communicate:
- deliberate construction;
- increasing functional stability;
- continuity with human material practice;
- a foothold becoming more inhabited through approved systems;
- practical resilience rather than triumphal conquest.

Human audio identity should generally favor:
- clear mechanical causality;
- readable start/stop/state transitions;
- moderate warmth and stability;
- tangible materials;
- bounded repetition;
- a sense that systems are built, maintained, and used by people.

It should not imply:
- a large unseen population;
- a restored civilization already complete;
- militarized colonial conquest;
- utopian certainty;
- an anthem declaring humanity's moral superiority.

The human sonic story is **capability returning**, not victory already achieved.

## 2.2 Survey gap

**APPROVED FOUNDATION**

The planet was surveyed before catastrophe, but available information is incomplete. Survey data may be technically useful while lacking lived, archaeological, ecological, or historical context.

**AUDIO GRAMMAR**

Sound should support the difference between:
- **expected environment** — readable natural/human categories;
- **encountered reality** — local details that complicate those categories.

Audio must not portray the survey as "wrong" merely because a location contains surprise.

Where future content authorizes a mismatch between expectation and encountered evidence, audio may introduce:
- an unexpected material resonance;
- a recurring relationship not present in ordinary wilderness;
- a local absence where ordinary activity would be expected;
- a cross-site sonic resemblance discovered only through comparison.

The mismatch should first read as **"this does not fit my current model"**, not **"the survey concealed a secret."**

## 2.3 Core theme

**APPROVED FOUNDATION**

The thematic question is:

> When given the chance to build civilization again from the beginning, should humanity restore the old civilization or build something better?

**AUDIO GRAMMAR**

Audio must not assign a "correct" sonic morality to preservation, restoration, adaptation, or reinvention.

Avoid a simplistic mapping such as:
- old = dark/dissonant/bad;
- new = bright/consonant/good;
- technology = cold;
- nature = morally pure;
- prior civilization = warning.

Instead, sonic contrast should focus on:
- continuity versus change;
- stable pattern versus altered pattern;
- inherited motif versus transformed motif;
- familiar material behavior versus locally adapted behavior;
- complete system versus selectively preserved system.

The same motif may remain recognizable after transformation. This supports the theme that continuity can survive change.

## 2.4 Sandbox-first narrative

**APPROVED FOUNDATION**

Narrative discovery is nonlinear, does not require one fixed ruin order, and must not subordinate the persistent sandbox to a mandatory main-quest sequence.

**AUDIO GRAMMAR**

No cross-phase sonic motif may require one discovery order to make sense.

Rules:
- a motif must remain locally coherent when first heard;
- later recurrence may add context but must not make the first use "wrong";
- audio cannot assume the player has heard an earlier occurrence;
- no "Act I / Act II / Act III" music progression may be required by chronology unless separately approved;
- no cue may function as a hidden mandatory quest gate;
- no final "truth theme" may override all prior environmental evidence.

# 3. FOUR PRIMARY SONIC CONTEXTS

These four contexts are distinctions for audio authorship. They are not game-state enums or runtime architecture.

## 3.1 Human settlement

**AUDIO GRAMMAR**

Sonic proposition:
**"People are building a durable foothold here."**

Characteristics:
- causal and legible mechanical action;
- compact state-change transients;
- warmer/stabler spectral center than exposed wilderness;
- controlled machine loops where approved active states exist;
- increasing density only when approved infrastructure actually exists;
- restrained musical support for resilience and continuity.

Relationship to #79:
- preserves the human foothold identity;
- extends it across future phases without turning it into a settlement anthem.

Must not:
- imply NPC activity not present;
- imply hidden automation;
- imply research or social systems that have not been approved;
- become universally "safe" if gameplay state says otherwise.

## 3.2 Wilderness

**AUDIO GRAMMAR**

Sonic proposition:
**"This world is alive, spacious, and not fully understood."**

Characteristics:
- broad environmental air;
- sparse organic detail;
- irregular natural rhythms;
- room for distance and negative space;
- locally specific density instead of constant spectacle;
- low informational certainty unless an approved event provides it.

Wilderness should not be the sonic opposite of civilization in a moral sense.

It can be:
- beautiful;
- indifferent;
- familiar in some ways;
- strange in others;
- safe at one moment and dangerous at another.

Must not:
- encode "alien = hostile";
- use constant ominous drones merely because the planet is unknown;
- announce hidden POIs or threats before approved detection.

## 3.3 Prior-infrastructure evidence

**AUDIO GRAMMAR**

Sonic proposition:
**"This was engineered, it is older than the current human foothold, and its full context is absent."**

Characteristics:
- material resonance with engineered regularity;
- sparse repeated relationships;
- traces of designed proportion without translating them into language;
- meaningful negative space;
- sounds of condition/process only when supported by visible or approved evidence;
- restrained continuity with #79 ruin direction: engineered, old, absent, unresolved.

The strongest signature is often not an exotic "alien sound." It is the tension between:
- evidence of design;
- evidence of history;
- absence of explanation.

Must not:
- become a faction anthem;
- resemble decoded speech;
- imply an active signal if no active system is approved;
- imply ritual/religion;
- imply magic/supernatural agency;
- imply a known function before evidence supports one.

## 3.4 Ambiguous anomaly contexts

**AUDIO GRAMMAR**

Sonic proposition:
**"The current model does not fully explain this."**

This context is not a license for horror language.

Use mismatch before spectacle.

Possible craft dimensions:
- familiar motif with one relationship displaced;
- expected resonance interrupted or extended;
- a material family that almost matches known prior infrastructure but does not align fully;
- spatial or temporal behavior that resists the currently established comparison set;
- absence of an expected layer;
- an unexpected overlap between natural and engineered sonic identities.

The anomaly should first signal **classification uncertainty**.

Must not:
- establish a new species/civilization;
- establish consciousness or intent;
- imply supernatural presence;
- become a dedicated "Something Older theme";
- imply cosmic horror;
- imply that the anomaly caused the Great Dispersal.

# 4. RECURRING MOTIF GRAMMAR

## 4.1 Relationship before meaning

**APPROVED MYSTERY STRUCTURE**

Repeated motifs may indicate relationship before their meaning is known.

**AUDIO GRAMMAR**

A recurring audio motif should answer:

> "Have I encountered something related to this before?"

before it answers:

> "What does this mean?"

A motif may be built from one or more abstract properties:
- interval or pitch contour;
- rhythmic proportion;
- attack/decay relationship;
- material resonance profile;
- density pattern;
- spatial repetition;
- timbral pairing;
- silence/response structure.

This document does not compose the motif or define instrumentation.

## 4.2 Motifs are not a dictionary

A recurring motif must not silently become:
- a translated word;
- a proper noun;
- a faction emblem;
- a religious symbol;
- an exact machine-function code;
- proof of authorship;
- proof of chronology.

If two sites share a motif, the audio claim is:

**"These may share a designed relationship or comparative feature."**

The audio claim is not:

**"They were built by the same faction for the same purpose."**

## 4.3 Recurrence strengths

Use recurrence strength to express confidence without declaring truth.

### Weak recurrence
Small shared property.

Player-facing implication:
"Possibly related."

### Family recurrence
Several stable shared properties.

Player-facing implication:
"Likely belongs to a recognizable design family or repeated relationship."

### Strong recurrence
Highly recognizable structural motif.

Player-facing implication:
"This relationship is deliberate enough to notice."

Even strong recurrence does not establish:
- identity;
- purpose;
- ideology;
- historical cause.

## 4.4 Variation rules

Variation may communicate:
- condition;
- age;
- reuse;
- later modification;
- incomplete state;
- local environment;
- context shift.

Variation must not erase motif recognizability when relationship is the intended evidence.

Conversely, a motif should not remain perfectly identical across every context if the environment shows meaningful historical layering.

## 4.5 Human/prior motif interaction

Human and prior-infrastructure motifs should remain distinguishable.

If future approved content calls for humanity to reuse or build around prior infrastructure, audio may layer the two identities rather than collapse them into one.

This preserves the thematic question:
- inheritance can be audible;
- adaptation can be audible;
- coexistence can be audible;

without declaring that humanity and the prior civilization share origin, ideology, or destiny.

# 5. SEVEN-THREAD SONIC LAYER RULES

The seven threads are an **APPROVED MYSTERY STRUCTURE**. The specific historical explanations inside them remain open unless separately approved.

Audio supports the structure by expressing evidence categories and interpretive tension, not by declaring the final answer.

## 5.1 Absence

Core sonic question:
**"What should be here, but is not?"**

Audio techniques:
- preserve negative space around engineered evidence;
- allow audible room/resonance without expected operational activity;
- use sparse residual material behavior rather than constant "ruin ambience";
- contrast a site's scale with low activity density;
- where evidence supports it, let repeated quietness become a cross-site observation.

What sound may establish:
- the place is not currently occupied in the expected way;
- audible activity is lower than the engineered context might suggest.

What sound must not establish:
- extinction;
- evacuation;
- global abandonment;
- a successful migration;
- a hidden surviving population.

Recontextualization example:
A site first heard as "empty" remains empty as observed evidence, while later comparison may make the quiet feel deliberate rather than catastrophic.

## 5.2 Dismantling

Core sonic question:
**"Does this read as broken, or as deliberately reduced?"**

Audio techniques:
- differentiate chaotic breakage from clean interruption;
- preserve a recognizable system family while omitting one expected layer;
- use bounded, non-destructive termination characteristics for controlled removal evidence;
- make removed/repurposed states quieter or structurally incomplete without using explosion/destruction language.

What sound may establish:
- current sonic structure is incomplete;
- a missing layer is repeatable across comparable evidence.

What sound must not establish:
- who removed it;
- why it was removed;
- whether removal was voluntary;
- whether it was preparation for departure.

Recontextualization example:
An initially sparse system identity may later recur at another site where the "missing" layer appears in a different context, strengthening a redistribution interpretation without proving it.

## 5.3 Infrastructure Network

Core sonic question:
**"Is this site self-contained, or part of a larger relationship?"**

Audio techniques:
- use motif families across spatially separate evidence;
- share proportions rather than identical full cues;
- allow different nodes to express different slices of one recognizable sonic grammar;
- emphasize relational consistency, not hidden active communication.

What sound may establish:
- multiple sites share designed relationships;
- local elements become more legible through comparison.

What sound must not establish:
- exact network function;
- active communication;
- planet-wide coverage;
- distributed intelligence;
- power, transport, computation, or ecological control unless separately evidenced.

Recontextualization example:
A local pulse initially heard as decorative may later match the spacing of another site's material response, reframing both as related design logic rather than isolated ornament.

## 5.4 Ecology

Core sonic question:
**"Is the environment merely near old infrastructure, or historically entangled with it?"**

Audio techniques:
- preserve a strong independent wilderness identity;
- allow correlation to emerge through repeated coexistence, not fusion by default;
- where approved evidence supports correlation, use subtle shared cadence or acoustic interaction;
- keep natural and engineered sources separable enough that causation is not falsely declared.

What sound may establish:
- natural and engineered evidence repeatedly coexist in a noteworthy way.

What sound must not establish:
- terraforming;
- ecological engineering;
- contamination;
- stewardship;
- beneficial/harmful intent;
- a single cause for all anomalies.

Recontextualization example:
A natural pattern heard near infrastructure may later occur without known infrastructure, weakening the assumption that the prior builders caused it.

## 5.5 Internal Disagreement

Core sonic question:
**"Do different places preserve incompatible choices?"**

Audio techniques:
- create divergent variants of a shared design family;
- layer later modification over an older recognizable base;
- let one site preserve a sonic layer another suppresses or bypasses;
- use difference in treatment, not "good faction/bad faction" musical coding.

What sound may establish:
- related evidence was handled differently across place/time;
- one modification conflicts with another design choice.

What sound must not establish:
- named factions;
- political ideology;
- war;
- rebellion;
- moral alignment;
- formal schism.

Recontextualization example:
A variant first heard as age/damage may later recur systematically by region, making "different historical choice" more plausible without proving political conflict.

## 5.6 Departure / Transformation

Core sonic question:
**"Does absence necessarily mean collapse?"**

**PROPOSAL / OPEN QUESTION guardrail:** The Great Dispersal remains a hypothesis label, not confirmed history.

Audio techniques:
- use continuity-through-reduction rather than mandatory destruction language;
- preserve motif identity while changing density, scale, or material carrier;
- where approved evidence supports transition, let one sonic family appear in altered context instead of disappearing completely;
- use deliberate cessation only when evidence supports controlled shutdown.

What sound may establish:
- some evidence is compatible with continuity despite reduced familiar presence;
- an old pattern may persist in changed form.

What sound must not establish:
- the Great Dispersal happened;
- the builders survived;
- they transcended;
- they migrated;
- they digitized themselves;
- they became ecological;
- any final transformation mechanism.

Recontextualization example:
A motif associated with built structures may later recur as a faint relational property in a different evidence context. The sonic conclusion is "possible continuity," not "they transformed into X."

## 5.7 Something Older

Core sonic question:
**"Does all evidence fit the comparison model currently attributed to the known prior-infrastructure family?"**

**PROPOSAL / OPEN QUESTION guardrail:** "Something Older" is only a hypothesis/category label. It is not a confirmed civilization, entity, intelligence, force, or chronology.

Audio techniques:
- avoid giving Something Older a canonical leitmotif;
- express mismatch through partial incompatibility with established prior-infrastructure grammar;
- permit ambiguity between natural, inherited, earlier-phase, or unrelated processes;
- use restraint: anomaly should be observable before explainable.

What sound may establish:
- this evidence does not fully match the current comparison set.

What sound must not establish:
- a second civilization;
- precursor species;
- intelligence;
- supernatural/cosmic agency;
- age beyond what evidence supports;
- relationship to the Great Dispersal;
- relationship to humanity.

Recontextualization example:
A mismatched resonance initially grouped under "prior infrastructure" may later remain physically identical while the player's classification changes. Audio must not have lied in the first presentation.

# 6. RECONTEXTUALIZATION GRAMMAR

## 6.1 Required four-layer model

Every cross-phase sonic reveal should be auditable against:

1. **Observed Sonic Fact** — what the player can actually hear.
2. **Natural Interpretation** — a reasonable early reading.
3. **Alternative Interpretation** — another explanation already compatible with the sonic fact.
4. **Later Recontextualization** — additional evidence that changes the preferred reading without invalidating the original sonic fact.

Audio should be authored so the **Observed Sonic Fact remains true** even after later context changes.

## 6.2 Recontextualization methods

### Method A — Same motif, new neighbor

Keep the motif stable; change the evidence around it.

Use when:
- new context should change interpretation without changing the original object.

### Method B — Missing layer restored elsewhere

A site lacks part of a sonic family; another site contains that layer.

Use when:
- Dismantling/Network comparison is intended.

Must not:
- declare why the layer moved.

### Method C — Variant reveals historical layering

A later site exposes a variant that makes an earlier difference meaningful.

Use when:
- Internal Disagreement or period change is plausible.

Must not:
- convert variation directly into faction identity.

### Method D — Natural/engineered overlap changes confidence

A sonic pattern occurs in both environmental and infrastructural contexts.

Use when:
- Ecology interpretation should remain uncertain.

Must not:
- make correlation equal causation.

### Method E — Motif survives context change

A relationship remains recognizable after density/material/space changes.

Use when:
- Departure / Transformation is being explored as a hypothesis.

Must not:
- establish survival or transformation as fact.

### Method F — Comparison set breaks

An anomaly fails to match the established family in a repeatable way.

Use when:
- Something Older is a useful interpretive question.

Must not:
- establish a separate civilization.

## 6.3 Contradiction through audio

Contradiction should target interpretation, not raw evidence.

Good contradiction:
- one site suggests controlled removal;
- another related site preserves the supposedly removed layer;
- the player must reconsider whether removal was universal.

Bad contradiction:
- the first cue unmistakably says "catastrophic destruction";
- a later cue says the same site was perfectly preserved;
- no new evidence explains the discrepancy.

# 7. NEGATIVE SPACE AND SILENCE

Silence is an authored narrative tool.

Use silence/low density to communicate:
- absence;
- scale;
- uncertainty;
- incomplete function;
- room for comparison;
- lack of explanatory certainty.

Silence must not automatically mean:
- death;
- horror;
- supernatural threat;
- hidden predator;
- memorial;
- sacredness.

A prior-infrastructure site can be quiet because its users are absent, not because audio is instructing the player to fear it.

Human settlement can become denser as approved systems exist, but should retain headroom so wilderness, mystery evidence, and critical gameplay cues remain legible.

# 9. CROSS-THREAD COEXISTENCE

One site may support multiple approved mystery threads.

Audio should not assign one cue per thread as though threads are mutually exclusive runtime states.

Instead:
- use a base context identity;
- add only evidence-supported sonic relationships;
- preserve uncertainty when several interpretations fit;
- prefer layered evidence over a stack of explanatory stingers.

Example:
A prior-infrastructure site may be:
- quiet enough to support Absence;
- missing one recurring layer, supporting Dismantling;
- share a motif proportion with another site, supporting Network comparison;
- sit inside a distinct ecological acoustic context.

The audio result should feel like one coherent place with several observable relationships, not four quest notifications.

# 11. DEEP LORE GUARDRAIL

Everything below remains **PROPOSAL / OPEN QUESTION / DEFERRED** unless separately approved.

Audio must not canonize:

## Prior civilization
- identity;
- species/biology;
- name;
- language;
- religion;
- ideology;
- political structure;
- final fate;
- relationship to humanity.

## Great Dispersal
- whether it occurred as one coherent process;
- cause;
- chronology;
- destination;
- success/failure;
- whether it was migration, decentralization, transformation, dismantling, shutdown, or multiple unrelated processes.

## Something Older
- whether it exists as a distinct historical category;
- whether it is artificial;
- whether it is intelligent;
- whether it is another civilization;
- whether it is natural;
- whether it influenced the prior civilization;
- whether it caused or influenced any dispersal/transition.

## Humanity
- exact catastrophe cause where unresolved;
- exact Seed Colony mission history where unresolved;
- exact status of other human survivors;
- final/canonical civilizational choice.

Audio may support uncertainty, inheritance, rebuilding, and thematic contrast. It may not answer these questions.

# 12. FUTURE AUDIO AUTHORING TEMPLATE

Any future cross-phase sonic narrative deliverable derived from this grammar should record:

- **SOURCE EVIDENCE:** approved narrative/world/design fact being supported.
- **THREAD RELATION:** one or more of the seven approved mystery threads, if relevant.
- **OBSERVED SONIC FACT:** what the cue/layer actually communicates.
- **NATURAL INTERPRETATION:** likely first reading.
- **ALTERNATIVE INTERPRETATION:** another compatible reading.
- **LATER RECONTEXTUALIZATION CAPACITY:** how future evidence can change interpretation without making the cue false.
- **MOTIF RELATION:** none / weak recurrence / family recurrence / strong recurrence.
- **CONTEXT:** human / wilderness / prior infrastructure / ambiguous anomaly / mixed.
- **MUST NOT:** unresolved truths the sound may not imply.
- **GAMEPLAY PRIORITY:** if applicable, inherited from approved gameplay/audio source.
- **AUTHORITY NOTE:** audio direction only; no runtime or canon invention.

This is a content-authoring record, not a runtime schema.
